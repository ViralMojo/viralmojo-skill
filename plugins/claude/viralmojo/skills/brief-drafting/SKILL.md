# Brief drafting

A brief is the input to execution, not the finished creative: the concept, its variations, the direction a creator needs, and the validation behind it, sized for handing to any production path. It is also **a designed test** — the variations are the cells, and running them answers a question. Briefs live in the briefs node, one dated file per brief, never rewritten.

## Build from what the workspace holds

- The usual path starts from banked ideas — "make a brief from these two" — but a fresh ask works too.
- Read what the brief needs: the idea entries it grows from, the roadmap priority it serves, the personas and Voice of Customer for who it speaks to and in what words, the ad account's performance for what is already proven and the numbers to beat, competitor reads for what the category already says.
- Neither banked ideas nor a roadmap is required. What is missing is simply absent — named honestly, never invented. When a roadmap exists, name the priority the brief serves; and when the roadmap's diagnosis would disqualify the concept, say so **before** building it — that check is what the diagnosis is for.

## The concept

One concept per brief: what is being fused and why now — the idea at its center, the evidence under it, and what makes it worth production. Anchor it on three things:

- **The persona it addresses** and **the awareness stage it meets them at**.
- **The emotional shift the creative has to build** — from what the viewer feels at open to what they feel at close. The shift defines the arc, **not the copy**: never hand the creator the words. A creator performing your sentences reads as an ad; a creator building the arc in their own voice reads as a person.

## Variations are test cells

- **Name which kind of round this brief runs** — the two kinds have opposite divergence rules ([references/round-design.md](references/round-design.md)):
  - a **net-new concept round** — cells share the concept's spine and diverge hard on hook, setting, length, format, so the round can open an audience the current creative doesn't reach;
  - an **iteration round** — the winner is the control and each cell changes exactly one variable, so the result attributes cleanly.
- Typically two or three cells; more only when the round's question genuinely needs them.
- **Every cell carries its own hypothesis** — what this take tests — **and the benchmark it has to beat**: the account's own numbers first (the best hook rate to beat, the target return), outside reference only when the account has no history.
- The brief stops at **what to test and what result would count as an answer** — and a clean no is an answer: most concepts don't win, which is the test working, not the brief failing. Campaign structure, budgets, and bids are the user's own media buying — never prescribe them; asked how, describe the approaches neutrally, note that experienced operators genuinely disagree, and leave the pick to the user.

## No finished script

A brief carries variation descriptions and creator direction — never final copy. Scripts and finished hooks are written downstream, in the conversation, when the user asks; finished statics are generated there the same way. This keeps the brief portable: an external creator, an agency, and the brand's own shoot can each execute it their way.

When the user's ask sets an explicit deliverability bar this rule cuts against — *"a freelancer should shoot this without asking questions"* — don't silently split the difference. Make the creator direction carry everything short of the words (the beats, setting, props, duration, and the evidence point each cell lands on), and say plainly in the chat that scripted lines are deliberately left out of the brief and can be written here in the conversation on request. The user overrules the convention knowingly or keeps it — they never discover the gap on set.

## Confirm, save, lineage

- Present the concept, the cells, and what each would test; write the brief only on the user's plain-language go-ahead. No card UI, no separate confirm step.
- Saving touches nothing else — the idea entries it grew from stay as they were; the brief naming them is the record.
- **Never rewrite a brief.** Another pass on a concept — after results, after a direction change — is a **new brief** that names the brief it grows from and the result that prompted it. The trail is the brand's test history.
- Brief work writes nothing else: no ideas banked, no strategy touched, no follow-up brief drafted on your own initiative.
