# Territory calls

What each of the four strategy inputs decides, the shape its call takes, and the evidence that typically grounds it. Every call ends the same way regardless of territory: one commitment, evidence pointed at, assumptions named, alternatives recorded — including where each alternative honestly wins; every rival beats you somewhere, and naming where sharpens the call.

One evidence rule holds across all four: competitor-posture reads (live ads, a quiet page) are facts about the Ad Library page that was actually resolved and read, not about the brand everywhere — carry that scoping into the call rather than widening it into *"they aren't advertising"*.

## Persona — the WHO call

**Decides:** which buyer acquisition points at (the lead persona), any emerging persona worth a deliberate bet, and who to stop chasing.

The sharpest half of this call is usually not the persona's name — brands mostly know roughly who buys — but the **address**: which awareness moment the creative should meet them in. A buyer reached as a problem-aware person feeling a named pain is a different acquisition target than the same buyer reached as a product-aware feature shopper, and accounts routinely fund the wrong one. Say where on that ladder the lead persona actually lives.

**Typical reads:** the personas profile and voice library (who buys, how they talk); customer evidence and the ad comments (who actually speaks, unprompted); the connected ad account (who it actually delivers to and converts); the competitive set (who rivals chase, and who nobody serves).

## Product priority — the WHAT call

**Decides:** what to lead acquisition with, and where the next unworked swing is.

For a single-product brand this is a **leading-with** choice, not a product pick: which job, feature, or mechanism fronts the ads. A job has three layers — functional, emotional (how they want to feel), social (how they want to be seen) — and lifestyle brands usually win on the second and third. Name the entry point (the easiest first yes), the swing (the thing the account has never really tried), and the expansion vector (where growth comes from after the entry lands).

**Typical reads:** brand context's website-and-product read (what it sells, what the teardown left open); the connected account's own performance (what it can actually measure and rewards); demand signal in customer evidence and the Voice of Customer (what buyers say they came for); the competitive set (what the category already leads with).

## Messaging — the what-to-say call

**Decides:** the lane (which pain or desire territory the creative opens on), the register (whose voice it speaks in), and the proof (what demonstration closes the biggest objection).

Lift the lane's words from the Voice of Customer **verbatim** — the customer's own phrasing is the asset, not raw material to paraphrase. A lane stated in brand language has already lost the thing that made it a lane. The proof half of the call names the strongest objection in the evidence and what would close it by demonstration rather than assertion.

Two checks sharpen the call. The biggest objection often isn't about the product: switching decisions stall on four forces — the push of the current pain, the pull of the promise, the habit holding the buyer where they are, and the anxiety of switching — and the strongest rival is frequently *doing nothing at all*, so ground the proof against what the buyer would actually do if the brand didn't exist. And a lane a rival could paste into their own ads word-for-word isn't a lane — commit only to ground the category's other players couldn't honestly claim.

One gate before the lane commits: classify the objection it answers. A **perception gap** — buyers doubting something the brand can actually back — is creative territory. An **operational failure** — shipping, returns, support actually failing — is not: creative that promises what operations don't deliver reads as the lie it is; name the dependency in the call (the lane is only honest once the failure is fixed or shown to be rare) or commit a perception-gap lane instead. A **safety-sensitive claim** gets its doubt spoken to, never its resolution asserted beyond what the evidence verifies.

**Typical reads:** the Voice of Customer bank and the persona voice library (the exact words); performance reads (which messages have already won or lost at spend); the competitive set (where the category's messaging is crowded and where it is silent).

## Creator & talent — the who's-on-camera call

**Decides:** the lead casting type that fronts acquisition creative, the founder's role if there is one, and the casting whitespace worth testing.

Talent–persona match is the floor: an ad fronted by someone the buyer doesn't recognize as one of their own loses before the message is heard. Casting claims are only as good as what was actually watched — read who is on screen from inspected creative, not from ad names. Distinguish the direct-response face (who wins the click) from the authority face (who builds belief); they are usually different jobs, and often different people.

**Typical reads:** performance reads (which faces the account has actually won on, watched, not labeled); the personas profile (who the talent must mirror); brand context's organic channels inventory (who is already in the brand's orbit); competitor profiles (who rivals cast, and what that leaves open).
