# AI writing tells — and how to check for them

Shared across every creative-writing deliverable — hooks, headlines, scripts, copy. The check is the same regardless of what's being written: does this sound like a person from this brand, or like a model.

## The tells

- **Colon-summary cadence.** Naming a concrete thing, then restating it as an abstract noun after a colon — "The result: confidence." "The difference: control." Real speech doesn't narrate its own point like that.
- **"Discover / Unlock / Elevate / Transform"-style openers.** Verbs that promise a vague upgrade instead of naming the actual thing that changes. Say what happens, not that something "unlocks."
- **A triad invented for rhythm, not because the brand thinks in threes.** "No bloat. No crash. Just clean energy." can be a genuinely strong structure — but only when it's carrying three real, distinct claims. The tell is reaching for the three-beat shape to sound punchy when there are only one or two real things to say, and padding a third.
- **False agency.** An inanimate subject doing a human's verb — "the data tells us," "the formula knows," "the results speak for themselves." Data sits there; someone reads it. Name the person, or drop the frame.
- **Negative-listing buildup.** "It's not a diet. Not a supplement. Not willpower. It's…" — drama manufactured by listing what the thing isn't before saying what it is. State the thing. One real exclusion answering a real objection is fine; the tell is the striptease.
- **The telegraphed reversal.** "X isn't the problem. Y is." "It's not about X — it's about Y." A formulaic pivot that sounds insightful without being specific. State Y directly and let it carry its own weight.
- **Hedge phrases.** "Say goodbye to," "the secret to," "at the end of the day," "when it comes to." These soften a claim that should just be stated.
- **Dictionary words where the brand's own customers use slang.** If the voice of customer says "gross" and the copy says "unpleasant," the copy is already off.
- **Marketing abstraction standing in for the named thing.** "Elevate your routine" instead of "get out the door two minutes faster." Name the concrete outcome; don't gesture at an improved state.

## The check: read it next to the real thing

Don't judge a candidate against a generic list — judge it against this brand's own real material. Pull two or three genuine examples: the account's own top-performing hooks or headlines, a monitored competitor's longest-running hook, or a straight voice-of-customer quote. Read the candidate next to them, out loud. If the real examples sound like a person talking and the candidate sounds like a brand, the candidate loses — rewrite it, don't polish it.

## The testimonial trap

"Best ever," "so good," "worth it," "love it," "can't believe how X" — these are things a customer says about a brand, not things a brand says about itself. They're right at home as a voice-of-customer quote and wrong the moment they're written as the brand's own copy. If a line reads like it belongs in a five-star review, it isn't a hook or a headline.

## Cold-audience-first

A line that only lands because the viewer already knows and likes the brand won't scale — most of the audience any of this reaches is meeting the brand for the first time. Check every hook, headline, and open the same way: does this work with zero prior context, or does it depend on the viewer already being a fan? If it only works warm, it doesn't work.

## No fabricated numbers or claims

The house rule already covers this — never invent. A stat, a study, a "doctors recommend," a count of anything: if it isn't traceable to something the workspace actually holds, don't write it in as fact. Say the claim is missing a source rather than inventing one to fill the gap.

## Compliance is a wall, not a suggestion

When the brand has said a term, a claim, or an angle is off-limits — a regulatory constraint, a category rule, something brand context records as "what it would never say" — that holds even if the user asks for it directly. Push back, say why, and offer the compliant version instead of writing the line anyway.
